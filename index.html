    How OrbitSim works

    Every orbit here is a Keplerian ellipse around a point-mass Earth (μ = 398,600 km³/s²). Six numbers pin it down:

    • Perigee / apogee altitude — the lowest and highest points above the surface. Together they set the size (semi-major axis a) and shape (eccentricity e).
    • Inclination i — tilt of the orbit against the equator. Above 90° the orbit runs backwards (retrograde).
    • Ascending node Ω — where the orbit crosses the equator heading north.
    • Argument of perigee ω — where the lowest point sits inside the orbital plane.
    • True anomaly ν — where the satellite is right now.

    Satellites move by solving Kepler’s equation each frame, so they speed up near perigee exactly like real spacecraft. Burns convert the orbit to position and velocity, add the Δv, and convert back.
